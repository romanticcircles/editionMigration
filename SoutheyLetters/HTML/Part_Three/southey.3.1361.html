1361. Robert Southey to Samuel Reid, 20 September 18071

My dear Reid

Your letter has slipt my memory by slipping under some papers out of sight – I beg your pardon for this very unintentional neglect.

There is little or no chance of procuring any of the books you mention in England. A copy of the Partidas2 was wanted for the defence of that scoundrel General Picton,3 & his council after long search were glad to xxx meet with one in Heber’s library. I have one & should be glad if I could spare it, – but it is of such daily use that this is impossible. If Dr Toulmin4 pleases I will, with great pleasure, write to my Uncle to procure the books from Madrid, where, & where only, they are likely to be had, –but there is great reason to fear that before such a commission could be executed xxx xxx xxx xxx, either Spain or Portugal will be at war, or the English expelled from Lisbon this latter I confidently expect, as one xxx consequence of our buccaneering attempts on South America.

I have to thank you for the trouble you took with my packages, all of which xxx arrived in excellent order. 5 Edith & Mrs Lovell beg to be remembered to you & your mother – so does Tom – I have a little girl & little boy whom I hope you will one day be prevailed upon to form an acquaintance with & be introduced to Derwentwater & Skiddaw at the same time

yrs very truly Robert Southey

Keswick.Sept 20. 1807

2.   Southey owned Gregorio López de Tovar (1496–1560), Las Siete Partidas del Sabio Rey Don Alonso el Nono Glosadas (1789). Back

3.   Lieutenant General Sir Thomas Picton (1758–1815; DNB) was convicted in 1806 of responsibility for the torture of a fourteen year-old slave girl while governor of Trinidad. Back

4.   Joshua Toulmin (1740–1815; DNB), Unitarian minister. Back

5.  Reid, who lived in Liverpool, had agreed to look out for several cargoes of books which had been sent to Southey by sea; see Southey to Charles Danvers, 20 April 1807 (Letter 1308) and Southey to John Theodore Koster, 20 August 1807 (Letter 1353). Back
