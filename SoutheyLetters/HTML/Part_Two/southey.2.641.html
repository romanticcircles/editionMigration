641. Robert Southey to John Rickman, 19 December 18011

Saturday. 19 Dec. 1801.

Yesterday your note arrived. this morning I have executed your commission – tho had not Xmas been so close at hand I should probably have presented a remonstrance against the wretched calvinistical cant of Hannah More – & recommended any thing instead – any chance number in Mr Lanes2 catalogue of novels in preference.

When I called with Allyballycarrickoshauglin3 at the Cockpit4 I immediately discovered the Goul.5 I wish his picture were before me – it would call up uglier phantoms than Fuseli6 can create xx by digesting raw pork.

My comforts are sadly lessened here. my Mother is very ill – she will hardly live out the winter. so much of our time & attention is necessarily taken up in a sick room – that little is left for any thing else. – xxxx xxxxxx I continue unemployed – except in attending Walkers Lectures.7 in all probability – from being no secretary at all I shall soon be transmuted into a sort of semi-tutor. Corry is familiar & friendly. but it is certainly odd that he has never asked me to dinner.

farewell. yrs R S.

Notes

1.  MS: Huntington Library, RS 18. ALS; 2p.
Previously published: Kenneth Curry (ed.), New Letters of Robert Southey, 2 vols (London and New York, 1965), I, pp. 264-265. Back

2.  William Lane (1745/6-1814; DNB), publisher of light literature and promoter of circulating libraries. Back

3.  The bog which Castle Rackrent looks out over in Maria Edgeworth (1768-1849; DNB), Castle Rackrent (1800). Southey was presumably calling in on Rickman to leave the copies of Castle Rackrent that Rickman had ordered as gifts; see Rickman to Robert Southey, 5 December 1801, in Orlo Williams, Lamb’s Friend the Census Taker. Life and Letters of John Rickman (Boston and New York, 1912), pp. 67-68. Back

4.  Government offices opposite Whitehall, used by the Privy Council office, where Rickman was directing work on the 1801 census. Back

5.  ‘The Goul’ was a nickname for a Mr Simonds (or Simmonds), who worked for Rickman on the 1801 Census; see Rickman to Robert Southey, 7 November 1801, in Orlo Williams, Lamb’s Friend the Census Taker. Life and Letters of John Rickman (Boston and New York, 1912), p. 58. Back

6.  Henry Fuseli (1741-1825; DNB), Swiss-born artist, whose work includes ‘The Nightmare’ (1781). Fuseli was rumoured to eat raw pork before sleeping in order to stimulate nightmares. Back

7.  Probably given by Adam Walker (1730/1-1821; DNB), famed for his lectures, especially on astronomy. Back
