3862. Robert Southey to [Isaac D’Israeli], 29 June 18221

My dear Sir

I am much obliged to you for your Illustration of the Literary Character,2 which is, like all your works, full of interesting matter agreably arranged. Here too you draw from yourself when you enlarge upon the advantages & comforts derived from a love of literature. When you treat of the Calamities & the Quarrels of Authors3 you happily write of evils in which you have had no share.

If inclination should ever lead you to the Lakes, I should have great pleasure in showing you my stores. You have seen a great deal of literary men in towns, but, for one class at least, the country is the proper place. You know what Cowley has said

Rura laudamus merito poetae;Rure floremus; dominoque laurumSole gaudentem necat oppidorumNubilus aer.4

You should look at us in our nests.

Present my compliments to Mrs & Miss D’Israeli,5 &

believe me My dear Sir with sincere respect Yrs faithfully Robert Southey.

Keswick.29 June. 1822.
